Hoarseness

Hoarseness is one of the most common reasons patients seek evaluation from a laryngologist. Hoarseness may include a rough voice, breathiness, vocal strain, reduced vocal endurance, changes in pitch, or difficulty projecting the voice.

While many cases are temporary, persistent hoarseness may indicate an underlying voice disorder that deserves evaluation by a voice specialist.

Request an Appointment

Common Symptoms

  • Raspy or rough voice
  • Breathy voice
  • Voice fatigue
  • Loss of vocal range
  • Difficulty projecting the voice
  • Needing increased effort to speak
  • Voice changes that persist for weeks

Common Causes of Hoarseness

  • Vocal fold nodules
  • Vocal fold polyps
  • Vocal fold paralysis
  • Laryngopharyngeal reflux
  • Voice overuse or misuse
  • Vocal fold atrophy
  • Benign vocal fold lesions
  • Neurologic conditions affecting the voice
  • Laryngeal inflammation

When Should You See a Specialist?

Patients should seek evaluation if hoarseness lasts longer than several weeks, significantly affects communication, is associated with swallowing difficulty, or develops without a clear explanation.

Professional voice users such as singers, teachers, attorneys, clergy, broadcasters, and public speakers may benefit from evaluation even when symptoms appear mild.

Evaluation of Hoarseness

Evaluation begins with a thorough history and examination. Specialized testing may include visualization of the vocal folds and assessment of vocal function to identify the underlying cause of voice changes.

  • Comprehensive voice evaluation
  • Videostroboscopy
  • Assessment of vocal fold motion
  • Evaluation of swallowing when appropriate

Treatment Options

Treatment is individualized and depends on the underlying diagnosis.

  • Voice therapy
  • Medical management
  • Office-based procedures
  • Treatment of vocal fold paralysis
  • Microlaryngeal surgery
  • Management of reflux-related symptoms
