Hoarseness

Hoarseness is one of the most common reasons patients seek evaluation from a laryngologist. Hoarseness can mean many things and, often, "I lost my voice" can mean something different to each of us. Voice changes may mean: roughness, raspiness, breathiness, vocal strain, increased vocal effort, vocal fatigue, reduced vocal endurance, changes in pitch, or difficulty projecting the voice.

While many cases are temporary, persistent hoarseness lasting longer than 2 weeks may indicate an underlying voice disorder that deserves evaluation by a voice specialist.

Common Symptoms

  • Raspy or rough voice
  • Breathy voice
  • Voice fatigue
  • Loss of vocal range
  • Difficulty projecting the voice
  • Needing increased effort to speak
  • Voice changes for longer than 2 weeks

Common Causes of Hoarseness

  • Vocal fold nodules
  • Vocal fold polyps
  • Vocal fold paralysis
  • Voice overuse or misuse
  • Vocal fold atrophy
  • Benign vocal fold lesions
  • Neurologic conditions affecting the voice
  • Laryngeal inflammation

When Should You See a Specialist?

Patients should seek evaluation if hoarseness lasts longer than 2 weeks, significantly affects communication, or develops without a clear explanation.

Professional voice users such as singers, teachers, attorneys, clergy, broadcasters, public speakers, salespeople, and many more may benefit from evaluation even when symptoms appear mild.

Evaluation of Hoarseness

Evaluation begins with a thorough history and examination, which includes visualization of the vocal folds and assessment of vocal function to identify the underlying cause of voice changes.

  • Comprehensive voice evaluation
  • Videostroboscopy
  • Assessment of vocal fold motion
  • Evaluation of vocal fold mucosal wave

Treatment Options

Treatment is individualized and depends on the underlying diagnosis.
