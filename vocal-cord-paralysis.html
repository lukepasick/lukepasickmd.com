Vocal Cord Paralysis

Vocal cord paralysis occurs when one or both vocal folds are unable to move normally. This condition can affect speaking, swallowing, breathing, and overall quality of life.

Dr. Luke Pasick specializes in the evaluation and treatment of vocal cord paralysis and other disorders affecting the voice, airway, and swallowing function.

Request an Appointment

Common Symptoms

  • Hoarseness
  • Breathy or weak voice
  • Difficulty projecting the voice
  • Vocal fatigue
  • Coughing while eating or drinking
  • Swallowing difficulty
  • Shortness of breath

Causes of Vocal Cord Paralysis

Vocal cord paralysis may occur for a variety of reasons, including:

  • Surgery involving the neck, chest, thyroid, or spine
  • Viral illness
  • Neurologic conditions
  • Trauma
  • Tumors affecting the nerve that controls vocal fold movement
  • Idiopathic causes, in which no specific cause is identified

Evaluation

A comprehensive evaluation may include a detailed history, examination of the voice and throat, and specialized assessment of vocal fold function. Diagnostic testing is tailored to each patient's symptoms and medical history.
