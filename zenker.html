Zenker Diverticulum

A Zenker diverticulum (or "Zenker's") is an outpouching that develops at the junction between the throat (pharynx) and top of the esophagus. Food, liquids, and saliva can collect in this pouch, leading to swallowing difficulties, regurgitation of food, bad breath, and other symptoms. Zenker's diverticulum most commonly affects adults over the age of 50 and often develops gradually over time.

What Are the Symptoms of Zenker's Diverticulum?

Symptoms vary depending on the size of the diverticulum but can include a range of the following:

  • Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)
  • Food becoming stuck in the throat
  • Regurgitation of undigested food hours after eating
  • Chronic coughing, especially after meals
  • Bad breath (halitosis)
  • Gurgling noises in the neck while swallowing
  • Recurrent pneumonia from aspiration

What Causes Zenker's Diverticulum?

Zenker's diverticulum develops when the upper esophageal sphincter, also known as the cricopharyngeus muscle, does not relax normally during swallowing. Over time, increased pressure during swallowing can cause the lining of the throat to bulge outward, creating a pouch.

The condition is generally not caused by cancer and is usually considered a benign disorder of swallowing function. Cancer associated with a Zenker diverticulum is exceedingly rare.

How Is Zenker's Diverticulum Diagnosed?

A thorough history and examination are the first steps in diagnosis. Symptoms such as regurgitation of undigested food, choking, and swallowing difficulty often raise suspicion for Zenker's diverticulum.

Modified Barium Swallow Study

The most common diagnostic test is a modified barium swallow or esophagram.

During this swallowing test:

  • A speech-language pathologist is present to perform the test.
  • The patient swallows liquids and foods containing barium.
  • Real-time X-ray imaging visualizes swallowing.
  • The diverticulum can often be seen filling with contrast material.

Flexible Laryngoscopy With or Without Stroboscopy

A small flexible camera may be used to examine the throat and vocal folds. This evaluation helps identify other conditions that may contribute to swallowing symptoms.

  • Fiberoptic Endoscopic Evaluation of Swallowing (FEES)
  • Esophagram
  • Esophagoscopy
  • High-resolution esophageal manometry

How Is Zenker's Diverticulum Treated?

Treatment depends on symptom severity, diverticulum size, overall health, and patient goals.

Observation

Small diverticula causing minimal symptoms may be monitored.

Endoscopic Treatment

Many patients are candidates for minimally invasive endoscopic treatment.

The procedure is performed through the mouth without an external incision and involves dividing the wall between the diverticulum and the esophagus in order to open the pouch. This improves drainage of the pouch and reduces resistance to swallowing. A newer endoscopic technique involves removing a portion of the pouch through the mouth.

Open Surgical Treatment

Some diverticula may require open surgery through a small incision in the neck. The pouch is isolated at the bottom of the throat and it is removed.
